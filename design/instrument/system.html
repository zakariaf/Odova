Odova · design system 1 of 3 · version 1.0 · 2 September 2026

Instrument

The car's own screen. Odova borrows its language from the instrument binnacle and the head unit: an unlit field with light drawn on it, tick marks, a gauge arc, a needle that can run past its stop, condensed numerals sized to be read at arm's length in a moving car. Elevation is luminance, never shadow. Colour is signal, never decoration — it appears only where the car itself would light a lamp.

For the driver who does not enjoy this. A commuter, a family, a plumber with two vans. They open the app two to six times a month, often at a pump in the rain, one-handed. They read one number and leave. So the number is enormous, the label is small, and the app answers before it asks.

It rejects: paper metaphors and stationery cues · serifs of any kind · warm cream and manila · soft rounded cards · drop shadows and floating layers · illustrations, mascots and celebration confetti · colour used to make a screen look designed. Nothing here is cosy. A dashboard is not cosy.

Spacing and radius

A 4px base. Screen gutter is --gutter 16px on every screen without exception; a row's vertical padding is --sp-2, a card's inner padding --sp-4, the gap between stacked cards --sp-3. 05

Elevation

There are no drop shadows in this system. A surface rises by getting lighter and by catching a 1px lit line along its top edge — the way a moulded plastic fascia catches the cabin light. The only box-shadow declarations in odova.css are the focus ring and the accent/state glow, which is light spilling out of a lit element, not a shadow falling under it. Screens must not add shadows; the screenshot tool photographs .device and anything bleeding past its edge is cropped.

06

Iconography

Drawn on a 24 × 24 grid, 1.5px stroke, round caps and joins, no fill except where an icon is deliberately solid (.icon--filled). Optical sizes are 16 / 20 / 24 / 28 via .icon--16 … .icon--28; the default is 20. Icons take currentColor and never carry their own colour. Every icon in the app is inline SVG written by hand — no icon font, no emoji.

Mirroring. Only icons that encode reading order or forward progress carry .icon--directional, and only those flip under [dir="rtl"] (via transform: scaleX(-1)). Everything that depicts a physical object or a rotating instrument keeps one canonical asset: dials do not run backwards in Tehran. Cells outlined in accent below are the mirroring set.

Motion

Instruments settle; they do not bounce. Everything except a needle uses --ease-standard. --ease-needle exists for one purpose: a gauge value or a large readout changing, where a slight overshoot reads as a physical pointer landing. Never use it on layout. All motion collapses to 1ms under prefers-reduced-motion, which is already handled in odova.css.

Rules of motion

Direction of travel is logical: a push enters from the inline-end edge and mirrors in RTL for free. The glow rises with --dur-base and never pulses — a blinking warning lamp is for a real fault, not for a UI affordance. Nothing loops.

09

The rules

Answer before you ask. Every screen opens with the number it exists to show, at readout size, with the unit small beside it. Labels are 10px tick captions. If a screen's biggest element is a control, the screen is wrong.

Colour is signal, never decoration. The chrome is neutral. A colour appears only when it carries state: --sig-overdue, --sig-due, --sig-due-soon, --sig-ok, --sig-unknown, --sig-needs, --sig-business, plus --accent for the one element that has the user's attention. Never colour a heading, an icon or a card to make a screen look designed.

Three signals, never one. Status travels on dot shape, colour and wording together. Set the state with one modifier on .due-card; it publishes --state and the sidebar, dot, status line, meter fill, chip and button all read it. Strip the colour out and the screen must still be readable.

Elevation is luminance. Step the surface (--surface-0 → --surface-3) and add the 1px --edge-lit top border. Never add a box-shadow — the only ones in the system are the focus ring and the accent glow, and nothing may bleed outside .device.

Plot instruments from the data. A gauge arc, a meter, a sparkline and a chart must be generated from the actual figures — 15,900 of 15,000 km is a needle past its stop, not a decorative flourish. If you do not have the number, do not draw the instrument.

Never guess in a way that looks like fact. An estimated odometer is prefixed ~ and set in .est; a projected date is fuzzy (“around mid-October”); an item whose history is unknown is .due-card--unknown, never overdue; a value that cannot be computed is .dash — an em dash. The tilde and the word “about” are the entire vocabulary. The distinction must survive colour and weight being stripped, and .est still holds 4.5:1.

No layout code says left or right. Use inset-inline-*, margin-inline-*, padding-inline-*, border-inline-*, text-align: start/end, and flex/grid, which mirror for free. Only icons that encode reading order — chevrons, arrows, backspace, undo — carry .icon--directional. Dials, pumps, wrenches, clocks and vehicle silhouettes keep one canonical asset and never mirror.

Bilingual by construction. Every text node that differs between locales carries data-en and data-fa, including numbers and dates: data-en="187,412 km" data-fa="۱۸۷٬۴۱۲ کیلومتر". Under [lang="fa"] Vazirmatn leads for text and numerals alike — never mix a Latin face with an Arabic one at different optical sizes.
