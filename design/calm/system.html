Odova · design system 1 of 3

Calm

Reassurance, in a small number of large things. Calm is the design language for the driver who does not enjoy this task and wants to be told it is handled: warm earth colour, few large rounded surfaces, one primary thing per screen sized much larger than everything else, and state carried by tone rather than alarm. Where a dense system shows eight rows, Calm shows three and a clear next action.

It is built for a commuter, a family, a used-car owner, a plumber with two vans — people who keep a car five years or more, open the app twice a month, and log at a pump in the rain, one-handed. Every target in this system is at least 52 px tall for that reason.

What Calm deliberately rejects. Dense tables. Hairline rules. Monospace figures. Dark chrome and instrument-panel metaphors. Tick marks, gauges and dyno charts. Siren red. All-caps labels. Text below 13 px. Pastel-fintech weightlessness — this is earth, not candy. And the most common mistake of all: treating “nothing is due” as an empty screen. In Calm that is the good state and it gets the best-looking screen in the set.
Both themes are drawn side by side throughout; this only flips the specimen page around them.

08

The rules

Instructions, not principles. If a screen breaks one of these, the screen is wrong.

One big thing per screen. Decide the single answer the screen exists to give, build it at .t-title-lg or larger inside a .card--lg or .due-card--primary, and let everything else be quieter and smaller. If two things look equally important, you have not finished designing.
Three, not eight. Home carries one primary card and at most two secondary ones, then a “See all” row. A list that could scroll forever gets a cap and a link, never a longer list. Air is the point: never reduce padding to fit another row in.
Never guess in a way that looks like fact. An estimated odometer is written ~187,400 km with .est — the tilde is part of the visible string, so the distinction survives colour being stripped out. A projected date is fuzzy (“around mid-October”). At confidence = default there is no figure and no date at all: the card reads Odova needs a reading to say when and its action becomes Update odometer. An item with no history is --unknown, never --overdue. A dash beats a plausible lie.
State is three signals, never colour alone. Dot shape is normative — filled ● overdue, ring ◉ due, small ● due-soon, hollow ◌ needs-odometer, outlined grey unknown — plus the state colour, plus wording that says the same thing. Overdue always uses a positive phrase (“Overdue by 900 km”), never a negative countdown.
Overdue is terracotta, not a siren. --color-overdue is a confident earth red used on a soft tint, at most once per screen. Do not stack red on red, do not add an exclamation icon to a red card, and never render eleven overdue items on a used car's first launch — they collapse into one .unknown-card.
“Nothing is due” is the good state. It gets .allclear — the sage wash, the largest mark on any screen, and a real second sentence (“Since the last oil change: 3,120 km · 4 months”). It is never an empty box with a grey icon, and it never nags.
Nothing tappable is under 52 px, and no text is under 13 px. Buttons are --touch-min tall or more, number-pad keys are 68 px, rows are 64 px. This app is used one-handed at a pump in the rain, so the primary action is always full-width at the bottom of the thumb's reach.
