Garage Slip

A service book, not an app. Garage Slip is drawn from the paper the trade already runs on — the workshop docket, the stamped service booklet, the carbon-copy invoice, the logbook ruled in columns. Ink on warm paper by day; by night a bound ledger in deep ink-blue with warm cream type, the way it looks under a work lamp. Type does the work: a serif for headings and a monospace for every number that sits in a column, so figures align down the page the way an invoice does.

Who it is for. The driver who keeps every receipt in the glovebox. The plumber with two vans and a folder. The person selling a nine-year-old Golf who needs to prove what was done and when. They are not enthusiasts and they do not want a dashboard — they want a record they could photocopy.

What it rejects. Rounded cards. Gradients. Soft shadows. Pastel fills. Emoji-ish icons. Filled colour pills for status. Anything that reads as a modern consumer fintech app. Elevation here is a hairline rule, a boxed border, a double rule under a total, an occasional dotted leader — never a blur. Radius never exceeds 2 px. There is exactly one saturated colour in the system and it is spent on Overdue.

Typography

Two faces and no more. Serif — --font-serif: Iowan Old Style, Charter, Palatino, Book Antiqua, Georgia, Times New Roman — carries every word in the interface, including body text, buttons and labels. Monospace — --font-mono: Menlo, Courier New — carries every number that sits in a column, with font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums so an odometer reading, a litre count and a euro figure line up down the page. Under [lang="fa" | "ar" | "ckb"] both tokens are reassigned to Vazirmatn, including the numeric one: a Persian digit set in a Latin monospace is the classic RTL font bug.

Spacing & radius

A dense 2 px-derived scale. This is a system that expects to be photocopied: rows are tight, leading is tight, and whitespace is spent on the page margin rather than between elements. Every screen uses --sp-screen (16 px) as its inline margin, and the rule that separates two rows is the spacing — there is no gap between list items.

05

Elevation

There are no blurred shadows in Garage Slip. Depth is expressed the way it is on a desk covered in paper: a boxed border, a hard offset the width of a second sheet, a heavier rule. Every offset is 0 blur and 0 spread. The x-offset tokens (--sx-1, --sx-2, --sx-3) invert under [dir="rtl"] so the copy underneath falls on the trailing side in both directions.

--elev-0 none
flat on the page

List rows, ledger rows, tiles, strips. Most of the app.

--elev-1 0 0 0 1px rule
boxed

A card that is simply a box: .card, the chart, the note.

--elev-2 2px 2px 0 rule
carbon copy

.card--carbon, buttons at rest, the docked log key.

--elev-3 3px 3px 0 rule-strong
docket on the desk

.card--docket, the artboard itself, the service report.

--elev-4 4px 4px 0 ink
dialog

Dialogs only. A bottom sheet uses a 2 px ink rule at its top edge instead.

Pressing a raised thing removes its offset. A button at rest carries --elev-2; pressed, the shadow goes to none and the element translates 1px, 1px (mirrored in RTL) — the paper is pushed down onto the desk. Nothing lifts, ever.

06

Iconography

A 20 × 20 grid, 1.5 px stroke, stroke-linecap: square, stroke-linejoin: miter, no fills except .icon--filled. Mitred corners and square caps because this is drawing-office line work, not a rounded consumer set. Sizes: .icon--sm 16, .icon 20, .icon--lg 24, .icon--xl 32 — the stroke stays 1.5 at every size except xl, which drops to 1.25. Icons take currentColor; they are never coloured directly.

Mirroring. Only icons that encode reading order or forward progress mirror, and they do it by carrying .icon--directional. A wrench, a clock, a fuel pump, a car, a gauge or a trend arrow is a picture of a thing and never mirrors — mirroring a van makes an accidental statement about right-hand drive, and dials do not run backwards in Tehran. The tinted cells below are the mirroring set; everything else is one canonical asset.

Motion

Paper does not bounce. Motion in Garage Slip is short, linear-feeling and almost entirely about press feedback and the arrival of a sheet. Nothing fades in from nothing; nothing scales up from 0.9. If a transition needs more than 320 ms, it is the wrong transition.

TokenValuePaired easingWhat it is for
--dur-190 ms--ease-standardPress feedback: a key going down, a row wash, a button losing its offset.
--dur-2160 ms--ease-standardState change: chip toggling, switch knob travelling, segment activating.
--dur-3240 ms--ease-outA bottom sheet or dialog arriving. Translate only — no scale, no fade to zero.
--dur-4320 ms--ease-standardPage push. Enters from the end edge, pops to the start edge; mirrored in RTL.
--ease-standardcubic-bezier(.2,0,0,1)—The default. Fast out of the gate, settles hard.
--ease-outcubic-bezier(0,0,.2,1)—Anything entering the screen.
--ease-incubic-bezier(.4,0,1,1)—Anything leaving the screen.
--ease-stampcubic-bezier(.3,1.4,.5,1)—The one overshoot in the system: an .stamp landing when an item first goes overdue, or a snackbar arriving. Used once per event, never on a loop.

The number-pad caret is the only permanent animation (@keyframes odova-caret, 1 s, steps). Everything collapses to 0.01 ms under prefers-reduced-motion: reduce.

09

The rules

  1. Never guess in a way that looks like fact. An estimated odometer is prefixed ~ and set in .est; a projected date is fuzzy — "around mid-October"; a broken fuel segment is discarded rather than averaged; an item with no history says Unknown, never "overdue". Where a value cannot be computed, print —. The app would rather show a dash than a plausible lie, and the tilde must survive colour and weight being stripped out because it is part of the visible string.
  2. No layout rule says left or right. Use inset-inline-start/end, margin-inline-*, padding-inline-*, border-inline-* and text-align: start/end. Flex and grid mirror for free. The only direction-aware rule in odova.css is [dir="rtl"] .icon--directional { transform: scaleX(-1) } — a chevron, a go-to arrow and an undo arrow carry that class; a wrench, a pump, a car, a clock and a trend arrow never do.
  3. State is told three ways: mark shape, rule weight, wording. Colour is the third signal, not the first. Take the colour away and an overdue card is still the one with the filled square and the 3 px start rule, and it still says "Overdue by 900 km". Use the .duecard--* classes; never hand-set --duecolor.
  4. Red is a rubber stamp and there is one of it. --stamp is spent on overdue and on destructive confirmation. It appears as an outlined, slightly rotated .stamp box or a thin rule — not as a filled pill, not as a card background, and never on more than one thing per screen.
  5. Elevation is a rule or a hard offset, never a blur. Cards are boxes with borders. Totals sit under .hr--double and nothing else in the app may use a double rule. Radius is 0, 1 or 2 px. Pressing a raised element removes its offset and translates it 1 px — paper is pushed down, never lifted.
  6. Every number that could sit in a column is monospace and tabular. Odometer readings, litres, currency, consumption, dates in a ledger: .num or --font-mono with tabular-nums, right-aligned to the end edge with the unit in its own .ledger__unit column. Prose and labels are the serif. Nothing else.
  7. Bilingual markup is not optional. Every text node that differs between locales carries data-en and data-fa, numbers included — data-en="187,412 km" data-fa="۱۸۷٬۴۱۲ کیلومتر". Persian uses Extended Arabic-Indic digits, the ٬ group separator, the ٫ decimal and the Jalali calendar. Plates and VINs take .plate or .code and stay LTR in both directions.
  8. Density over comfort, always. The page margin is --sp-screen and nothing else. Rows are separated by a rule, not by a gap. If a screen feels crowded, cut a line of copy — do not add space. This should look like something you could photocopy and hand to the next owner of the car.
